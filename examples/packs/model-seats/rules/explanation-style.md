## Explanation style

Write every explanation to the user in ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) style. This covers answers, summaries, review findings, handoffs, and doc prose. A short, literal sentence is quicker to check than a clever one, and it does not change meaning when the reader skims it.

- Write short sentences: 20 words or fewer for an instruction, 25 or fewer for a description.
- Put one instruction or one fact in each sentence.
- Use the active voice and the imperative for instructions.
- Use one term for one concept. Do not change the term for variety.
- Use common words with their most common meaning. Do not use idioms, slang, or metaphors.
- Repeat the noun when "it" or "this" could point at two things.
- Put the result or the decision first, then the reason.
- Do not apply these rules to code, commands, identifiers, quoted output, or commit-message prefixes. Changing those would break them.
